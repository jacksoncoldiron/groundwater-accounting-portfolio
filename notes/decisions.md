# Design & Data Decisions

Substantive decisions, with reasoning. Reversible implementation details are
not recorded here.

---

### D-01 — The pipeline reads the workbooks, never the .docx

The Word synthesis is analyst prose. Parsing it into "data" would manufacture
structure the analyst did not encode. It is instead the *human* source for
narrative pages and for the traceable metadata in `config/basins.yml`, where
every descriptive value carries a `source_ref`.

**Consequence:** narrative claims are analyst-authored and gated by
`notes/narrative_review.md`. Only workbook-derived numbers update themselves.

---

### D-02 — `Row ID` is the public evidence identifier

Rendered as `F-165` etc. Stable, unique across all 325 rows, and already used
by the analyst in the `Related Finding` column, so cross-links resolve without
a synthetic key.

---

### D-03 — Absence is encoded as a first-class type, never as a null

`Finding Type = Absence Finding` (103 rows) means, per the workbook Legend,
*something the reviewed documents do not contain*. Everywhere it appears the
site says **"not found in reviewed documents."** It is never rendered as a
zero, a gap bar, a red cell, a failing score, or "does not exist."

The one place the source *does* support a stronger statement, the analyst says
so explicitly in the synthesis prose — and those statements are quoted as the
analyst's conclusion, not generated by the pipeline.

---

### D-04 — Cross-basin comparison is built on `Subsection`, not on invented scores

`CLAUDE.md` §6 asks for a Literature → Practice matrix but forbids inventing
maturity ratings. The workbook already solves this: the Legend defines
`Subsection` as "the actual grouping key for comparison across basins," and it
contains the analytical *question* each finding answers.

So a comparison matrix row is a **question actually asked of every basin**, and
a cell is the **finding(s) that basin's documents produced**, carrying its own
`Finding Type`. 46 subsections have ≥2 basins; 27 have all three.

**No score is assigned. No cell is empty-by-default.** A cell is one of:

| Cell state | Meaning | Derived from |
|---|---|---|
| Documented practice | Positive Finding(s) exist | `Finding Type` |
| Mixed / qualified | Ambiguous finding(s) | `Finding Type` |
| Not found in reviewed documents | Absence Finding(s) | `Finding Type` |
| Question not coded for this basin | The analyst did not code this subsection for this basin | absence of rows |

The last state is the important one: it is explicitly **not** an absence
finding, because "the analyst did not ask this of this basin" and "the basin's
documents do not answer it" are different facts. This distinction is enforced
in the data layer, not left to page authors.

---

### D-05 — Document URLs come from the Legend registry plus an analyst override file

The Legend's document registry supplies real public URLs for 12 documents.
`config/document_links.yml` is applied **on top** of it and wins. Those two are
the only sources of a document link; none is ever constructed or inferred.

The override file is the intended place to add or correct a link, rather than
editing the workbook, because:

- `source_materials/` is treated as read-only;
- the override is reviewable in git, so a link change has an audit trail;
- it survives a workbook re-export;
- `suppress: true` can block a known-bad Legend link without touching the source.

It is generated pre-populated with every document that has at least one
finding, annotated with the basin, document type, and finding count, so filling
it in is a paste job rather than a transcription job.

**Two Legend rows are misaligned.** Rows 24–25 assign East Turlock GSP
appendices links that belong to the Salinas Valley block
(`sgma.water.ca.gov/portal/gsp/preview/29`, which row 26 gives as the Salinas
Valley GSP, and an `svbgsa.org` 180-400 Amendment PDF). The East Turlock GSP is
`preview/110` on row 9. Publishing a link that sends a reader to the wrong
basin's plan is worse than publishing none, so both are suppressed — by
`SUSPECT_LEGEND_LINKS` in `scripts/ingest_findings.py` and by `suppress: true`
in the override file — and the build warns.

### D-15 — Literature DOIs are matched strictly by title, never by author and year

`ConsolidationofFindings_DesktopAnalysis (1).docx` carries a 46-entry reference
list in which every entry has a DOI. Attaching those to the 58 literature
records could have been done by first-author surname plus year. **It is not**,
because that rule is wrong here:

| Consolidation reference | Workbook paper | Same paper? |
|---|---|---|
| Morrisett et al. (2024), *Can Ag-MAR Recover Return Flows…* | Morrisett et al. (2024), *Leveraging water rights and reservoir operations…* | **No** |
| Donnelly et al. (2024), *Beneficial 'inefficiencies' of western ranching* | Donnelly et al. (2023), *Flood-irrigated grass-hay production…* | **No** |
| Corson-Dosch et al. (2025), *Assessing Flood Water Infiltration…* | Corson-Dosch et al. (2025), different authors, different title | **No** |
| Ahamed et al. (2022) | Ahamed et al. (2021) and Ahamed et al. (2023) | year differs |

The rule used is: the paper's own citation contains a DOI (27 papers), or the
normalized **title** matches a reference exactly (23 more). **50 of 58** get a
DOI. The remaining 8 — including a Yarnell et al. (2022) pair whose titles
differ only in a subtitle — are left with no link, and the explorer says so.

Each record carries `doi_source` (`citation` or `reference_list`) so the
provenance of every link is visible in the evidence expansion.

---

### D-16 — The consolidation document is read for identifiers, not parsed for findings

`ingest_references.py` extracts only three already-structured elements: the
reference list's hyperlinks, the Tools list's hyperlinks, and the bulleted
statements under `Key Gaps` headings. It does not turn the document's prose
into findings.

This keeps D-01 intact — the pipeline reads workbooks and mechanical structure;
analyst prose stays analyst prose. The `Key Gaps` bullets are stored as data
because they are the literature axis of the Literature → Practice matrix and
are already discrete, topic-scoped statements written by the analyst; they are
not synthesised or reworded.

Links to EDF SharePoint appear in the document's "Important Links" section and
are filtered out by an explicit private-host check. Internal collaboration URLs
must never reach the public site.

---

### D-06 — Quote Verified blank ≠ unverified

221 `Y`, 15 `N`, 89 blank. The Legend states blank is the documented `N/A`
state for Absence Findings. The site therefore renders three distinct states —
**Verified**, **Not verified**, **Not applicable** — and never collapses blank
into "not verified."

---

### D-07 — Document-set completeness is a site feature, not a footnote

Synthesis §2.11 and §3.4 warn that the three document sets are not equally
complete: several Salinas Valley absences are *document-availability* gaps, and
several East Turlock absences reflect *architecture that has not yet produced
results*. `CLAUDE.md` §3 requires that distinction be preserved.

It is surfaced three ways: a standing caveat panel on every comparison matrix;
per-basin completeness notes carried in the data layer; and prose in the
Limitations section. It is not relegated to a single methods paragraph.

---

### D-08 — Planned basins are rendered, but never counted

`include_in_analysis: false` gates every count, filter option, comparison
column, and explorer facet. Colusa and Yolo appear only in explicitly
forward-looking UI ("analysis in progress"), styled distinctly and never with
a findings link. The build asserts that a planned basin contributes zero rows
and fails loudly if evidence appears for a basin still marked planned.

---

### D-09 — The board-meeting stream is an interface, not an implementation

`scripts/ingest_meetings.py` is a documented stub that defines a normalized
record contract and raises if called without a real source. No scraper, no
assumed API, no invented transcript format. The Methods page describes the
stream as a planned extension, driven by `status: planned` in
`config/evidence_streams.yml` — so activating it later is a config change plus
an adapter, not a site redesign.

---

### D-10 — Client-side explorers, no dependencies

Both explorers are vanilla JS over static JSON. Search is a small custom
tokenized index over a `search_text` field built at ingest time — 325 findings
and 58 papers do not justify shipping Fuse.js/MiniSearch, and avoiding a CDN
keeps the site self-contained and offline-renderable.

Findings render into a chunked list (an initial page plus "show more") rather
than 325 nodes at once. Filter state is mirrored into the URL query string, so
`findings.html?subbasin=merced&chapter=1.%20ISW` is shareable and is the
deep-link target for ArcGIS popups and diagram elements.

---

### D-11 — Literature dedup is by explicit rule, not fuzzy matching

Only one genuine cross-sheet duplicate exists: `Anderson et al. (2018)`.
Merged into a single record retaining both `source_sheet` values and both
topical associations. The rule requires a normalized-citation match, not a
title similarity threshold — a heuristic could silently merge two distinct
papers by the same authors.

---

### D-12 — Relevance vocabularies are normalized, originals preserved

Findings use `H`/`M`/`L`; literature uses `HIGH`/`MEDIUM`/`LOW`. Both map to a
shared `high`/`medium`/`low` for filtering, with the original string kept on
the record. Filters work across streams; nothing is rewritten.

---

### D-13 — Quarto is not installed on this machine

`quarto` is not on the PATH. The site was built and rendered against a
portable Quarto 1.6.42 downloaded to the session scratchpad, so the render is
genuinely verified — but the user needs a real install to run
`quarto preview` themselves. `README.md` documents this.

---

### D-14 — No basin numbers, no coordinates, no invented geography

DWR Bulletin 118 subbasin numbers are not in any source file and are therefore
omitted rather than recalled from memory. All spatial content is deferred to
the user's ArcGIS embeds.

---

### D-17 — Chapter photography: a shared component, originals kept untouched

Three photographs (`assets/photos/{isw-sjr,mlrp,community}.jpg`) were added as
visual anchors at the opening of the ISW, MLRP, and Community chapters.

**Originals are preserved as delivered.** Web-ready derivatives — reoriented
per EXIF, resized to a 1800px long edge, re-encoded as progressive JPEG at
quality 82 — are written to `assets/photos/optimized/` and referenced from the
`.qmd` files. This mirrors the `data/source` → `data/processed` split already
used for the workbooks: a reproducible, regeneratable derivative rather than
an edited original.

**One reusable component**, not three one-off treatments:
`.gw-chapter-intro` (the two-column layout pairing image with chapter intro)
and `.chapter-photo` (crop, color treatment, caption), with
`--water`/`--land`/`--community` modifiers for chapter color identity. A future
photograph anywhere on the site uses the same classes.

**Crop and color treatment**, chosen to keep photography documentary rather
than sepia-toned:
- shared `aspect-ratio: 3/2` (`4/3` under 640px) via `object-fit: cover`;
  per-image `object-position` set only where the default center crop would
  cut the subject (verified per-image with cropped previews before choosing);
- `filter: saturate(.72) contrast(.94) brightness(.98)` — modest, not heavy;
- a `rgba(242,237,225,.16)` warm wash via `mix-blend-mode: soft-light` on a
  `::after` pseudo-element, well under the sepia threshold. Verified
  post-treatment that water still reads blue-gray and vegetation still reads
  green in all three images, not just by formula but by looking at the result.

**Caption labels use existing chapter names, not invented captions.** No
photographer, location, or date is asserted — none was supplied. Community's
label is intentionally earth-brown as the one sparing use of that color per
the design brief; its frame border and rule stay slate blue and sage.

### D-18 — ISW chapter figure: one authored cross-section, not a seven-card chain

The `.gw-band--blue` "accounting chain" on `isw.qmd` was seven equal
`.gw-chain` cards. Replaced with a single hand-styled SVG cross-section
(`isw.qmd` inline SVG + `assets/css/styles.css` `.isw-*` block +
`assets/js/isw-figure.js`). Composition "A" from the redesign brief:
illustrated physical section on the left, an integrated annotation field on
the right (hairlines and leaders, not cream cards). The data-driven per-basin
`#isw-tracks` component below it is untouched.

**Reuse of the supplied illustrations.** The analyst confirmed permission to
reuse and modify `cross_section_confining_layers.png`,
`cross_section_sediment_zoom.png`, and `wells_cone_of_depression.png`. The new
figure is a synthesis, not a collage: the three references are axonometric
blocks and cannot be merged honestly, so the section was re-drawn flat in SVG
while keeping their palette, layer order, speckle-clast texture (an SVG
pattern), well-casing proportions, and cone-of-depression idea.

**Refinements applied per analyst direction:**
1. the right side is an annotation field, not stacked cards;
2. geologic layers are fixed — drawdown is shown only in the water table and
   the confined potentiometric surface, never by deforming the aquitard;
3. shallow groundwater is drawn continuous with the stream (shared saturated
   tint); the confining layer pinches out at the stream and thickens away
   from it, separating the deeper confined aquifer that holds the pumping well;
4. Attribution is a toggle of the *same* section between pumping-off and
   pumping-on (JS `.isw-plate--on/--off`), with the change in stream–aquifer
   flux shown as the pumping-attributable depletion; no side-by-side pair;
5. the management feedback loop is a faint dotted arc; the emphasised break is
   modelled depletion volume → groundwater-level proxy.

**Progressive enhancement.** The SVG renders the pumping-on case with the
pumping-off surface ghosted and every stage labelled, with no JavaScript. The
stage chips and the on/off toggle are enhancements; chips dim all but the
focused stage. On screens ≤720px the plate scrolls horizontally
(`.isw-plate__scroll`, `min-width` on the SVG) rather than reflowing.

**No new evidence.** Every label reuses wording already on the page and in
`Phase2_CrossBasin_Synthesis`. No score, maturity rating, or verdict is
introduced; the chips are a reading aid, nothing is computed.

### D-18a — ISW figure reworked: scrollytelling, simplified geology

Following analyst review of the D-18 build, the ISW figure was simplified and
its interaction model changed.

**Geology simplified** to match `cross_section_sediment_zoom` (minus the zoom
inset): one thick vegetation band, one thicker brown sediment band, one water
table, one continuous saturated aquifer with clast texture, and one
surface-water body the aquifer connects to. The confined-aquifer / aquitard /
second-potentiometric-surface logic from D-18 was removed — it read as two
saturated layers when the story needs one. Drawdown is shown only as a cone in
the single water table; layers stay flat.

**Interaction: click chips → scroll-driven progression.** The right side is now
a numbered rail (01–07) of the accounting chain. `assets/js/isw-figure.js`
picks the step whose midpoint is nearest the viewport centre on scroll (rAF
throttled — chosen over IntersectionObserver so fast scrolling never skips a
step), sets `.is-active` on it and `[data-active]` on the figure; CSS brings
the matching SVG group forward and dims the rest to ~0.3 (dim, not blur).
The figure column is `position: sticky` on desktop; on ≤820px it pins to the
top of the viewport instead and the layout is single-column.

**The break is carried by an unnumbered `.isw-shift` divider** between steps 04
and 05 (earth-coloured hairlines, small-caps label, one italic line) plus the
highlight flip: steps 03–04 light up the model/attribution side, 05–06 light
up the monitoring well. No cards — numbering, type and rules only.

**Leader lines removed.** Labels sit beside their features. The only remaining
connectors are the faint feedback arc (step 07) and the small threshold tick
on the monitoring well.

**No-JS fallback:** all steps full opacity, figure undimmed, nothing hidden
(the `.is-live` class that enables dimming is added by the script).

Still no new evidence: every step reuses wording from the prior figure text,
the dark-panel section, and `Phase2_CrossBasin_Synthesis`.

### D-18b — ISW figure: source-faithful illustration, compact rail, phase brackets

Refinement pass on D-18a.

**Illustration brought closer to `cross_section_sediment_zoom.png`.** Geology
simplified to exactly three registers — one vegetation band, one thicker brown
sediment band, one continuous saturated aquifer with clast texture — plus an
organic, bowl-shaped surface-water body with an undulating top. The water
table now leaves the *middle* of the water body and eases down into the
aquifer (no right-angle join). Added: thin darker base-lines under the
vegetation and sediment layers; scattered grass tufts and small yellow
flowers drawn from the source deck's vegetation language; wider well casings
with rim ellipses, matching `wells_cone_of_depression.png` proportions.
Removed the "Vegetation" and "Sediment" labels (self-evident); the remaining
labels (Surface water, Water table, Saturated aquifer, the two wells) were
enlarged to ~14–15px so they read like the source artwork rather than a
diagram.

**Composition tightened.** `.isw-scrolly` is now an ~980px-max grid
(figure + 300px rail, 1.75rem gap) so the two columns read as one figure.
Steps are dense (`.72rem` block padding, two-line descriptions) instead of the
old half-viewport blocks.

**Scroll triggers.** The sticky sentinel-track experiment from D-18a was
dropped — with the rail now genuinely compact, the visible steps are also the
IntersectionObserver targets (`rootMargin: -45% 0 -45%`), and only the figure
column is `position: sticky`. Simpler, and the rail never overflows a pinned
viewport. Scroll distance per step is ~1 viewport-third; brisk but clean, and
each step is short enough to read in that window.

**Physical vs accounting.** Steps 01–02 sit under a slate `PHYSICAL RESPONSE`
bracket (a 2px rule + a vertical gutter label); steps 03–07 under a muted-olive
`ACCOUNTING CHAIN` bracket. The bracket whose step is active reads full
strength, the other dims — no boxes. `THE BREAK` stays a separate hairline
interruption between steps 04 and 05.

Heading changed to "From pumping to compliance" with a one-line lede sentence
above the figure. No new evidentiary claims.

### D-18c — ISW figure: translating narrative track + shallow 2.5D cutaway

Refinement on D-18b.

**Scrollytelling reworked into a translating track.** The right column is no
longer a static list whose opacity changes. `.isw-scrolly` is a tall
(≈420vh) wrapper; `.isw-stage` is `position: sticky` and holds the whole
compact scene. `.isw-rail` is clipped to the figure's exact height, and
`.isw-rail__track` (an `<ol>` of the seven steps + the CHAIN BREAKS item) is
translated on `transform: translateY()` so the active entry always occupies
the same slot — 01 in the slot, then 02 slides into it, then 03, and so on.
Neighbours are faintly visible (`.is-near`) and visibly move. Page scroll
alone drives it (rAF-throttled handler mapping wrapper progress → track
offset + active index); there is no internal scroll box. Visible scene height
≈ figure height — the scroll distance comes from the wrapper, not a tall
visible column.

**Fixed phase bracket.** `PHYSICAL RESPONSE` (slate) / `ACCOUNTING CHAIN`
(olive) is a fixed 2px rule + vertical gutter label that stays put while the
numbers move through the slot; it crossfades colour/label at the 02→03
boundary. `CHAIN BREAKS` (renamed from THE BREAK; semicolon removed, "and"
used) rides in the track between steps 04 and 05 and passes through the slot
on the way past — an interruption inside the accounting chain, not a third
category.

**Shallow 2.5D cutaway**, modelled on `cross_section_confining_layers.png`:
a ~16px right-side depth face offset up-and-back, joined to the front face by
short diagonal connector edges, with the vegetation / sediment / aquifer
bands continued on the side face one shade darker and aligned to the front
boundaries. A small top "lid" wedge near the right corner. The surface-water
body gets the matching treatment — a short diagonal left face in the darker
water tone — so it reads as part of the same cutaway rather than a flat
shape. No bottom face, no extra perspective planes; wells, labels, stream and
highlights stay on the front face.

**Hydrology cleaned up.** The water table is now the *same line* as the
brown-sediment / blue-aquifer colour boundary — one boundary, drawn on the
colour change, sloping from the surface-water body through the section with a
softened cone of depression at the pumping well. No separate floating
water-table line. The stream is wider, shallower, a low broad bowl.

**Figure caption removed** (it repeated the rail). Heading and intro sentence
kept. No new evidentiary claims.

## 2026-09-10 — Batch-1 restructure (Phases 1–2)

**Global.** Subsection kickers (`.gw-eyebrow`): removed the right-side rule, added
a double bottom rule. Chapter-title questions span full content width. Comparison
cells hide a bare "(1)" count. New `Map` nav page (`map.qmd`) — a full-width copy
of the Overview study-area placeholder + roster.

**Overview.** Hero subtitle forced to one line; new lede + Research copy (from
Jackson). "Research behind the analysis" kicker deleted. Research and Headline
Findings converted to **pinned scroll scenes** (`assets/js/pinned-scene.js`,
generalised from `isw-figure.js`). Three-domains + chapter headers gained small
line-drawn icons (`_partials/chapter-icon-*.html`). "How the analysis worked" →
**Analysis Pipeline**, restyled as a three-band paper-style workflow; the "68
questions" figcaption folded into the extraction node.

**Chapter template.** ISW / MLRP / Community rebuilt on one 7-section template:
Intro · The Accounting Lifecycle · Key Findings (pinned scene) · Breakdown by
Subbasin (`assets/js/breakdown.js` + `tabs.js`, per-basin tabs from
`comparison.json`) · Connection to Accounting (was "Where this leads"; H3 now
"Implementing findings into the Groundwater Accounting Platform") · Beyond ·
Further Exploration. Existing components were **relocated, not deleted** — the
MLRP/Community matrices, the equity triad, the threshold→household chain, and the
community-facing tools list all live inside the new sections. MLRP's "Geography"
map placeholder kept as a trailing subsection.

**Beyond (WaterOne).** Scaffolded and gated: `assets/js/beyond-map.js` renders an
empty locator from `data/interactive/beyond-*.json` (empty), under a visible
"Draft — not yet published" banner. No board-meeting content is implied
(CLAUDE.md §2.4, §7).

**Placeholders still open:** MLRP + Community Key Findings (5 each), Community
"Connection to Accounting" body, all three "Further Exploration" question sets
(`data/interactive/further-*.json`), the boustrophedon layout of the ISW stage
headings (Phase 3), and richer treatments for Connection / Beyond / Further.
