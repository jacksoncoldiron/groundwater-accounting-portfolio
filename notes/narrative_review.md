## Part 1 — Verification of the candidate headlines in `CLAUDE.md` §21

`CLAUDE.md` asked that these be checked against the synthesis before they
appear publicly. All five were checked against
`Phase2_CrossBasin_Synthesis.docx`.

### ✅ C-1 — "Measurement is more sophisticated than accounting"

**Verdict: supported, and stronger than the brief's provisional wording.**

Synthesis §1.8 Comparison: *"All three basins show a compliance loop that
closes on a groundwater-level proxy rather than on any measured or modeled
depletion quantity, and none attaches numeric uncertainty to an ISW figure
specifically."*

Reinforced by §1.1 (all three run same-generation IWFM/MODFLOW-class numerical
models — "no real difference in model sophistication"), §1.2 ("None of the
three sets a threshold directly on a depletion volume"), and §1.3 (Merced and
East Turlock report their pumping-attributable figure "only as background
information").

**Safe public wording (scope-limited):** "In all three reviewed subbasins, the
compliance loop closes on a groundwater-level proxy rather than on a measured
or modeled depletion quantity."

**Do not** generalize past the three reviewed basins. **Re-review on any basin
activation** — this is an "all three" claim.

---

### ✅ C-2 — "Verification is the missing middle of land repurposing"

**Verdict: supported, with the per-basin differences intact.**

Synthesis §2.4 Comparison: *"Merced measures after implementation and the
number is enforceable. East Turlock estimates before implementation and never
compares the estimate against anything afterwards. Salinas Valley's most
careful measurement is made by a partner organization at a scale larger than
the subbasin."*

§2.2 Gaps: *"None of the three runs a paired comparison of expected against
measured savings on an enrolled parcel, which is the check the literature's
verification gap describes."*

**Required caveat (§2.11):** Salinas Valley's absences here are partly
*document-availability* gaps — the MLRP Plan, the siting tool specification,
and the demand-management framework were not available. East Turlock's are
partly *architecture without results* — no projects implemented, monitoring
plan still a draft. This caveat must travel with the claim.

---

### ✅ C-3 — "Allocation rules determine whether a water saving stays saved"

**Verdict: supported.**

Synthesis §2.3 Comparison: *"Merced pays an incentive, the landowner keeps the
land, and the entitlement stays with them and can move. East Turlock pays per
acre, the landowner keeps the land, and the entitlement is suspended for the
paid term with no transfer mechanism in existence. Salinas Valley buys the
land, and there is no entitlement to keep or forfeit."*

§2.3 Gaps: *"None of the three publishes any reporting on the allocation status
of repurposed parcels, so none could detect movement even where movement is
permitted."*

**Note the precision required:** East Turlock **suspends** rather than
**retires** the entitlement. Do not compress this to "retires."

---

### ✅ C-4 — "Community equity is not one variable"

**Verdict: supported, and the distributional half is the sharper finding.**

Synthesis §3.1.3 Comparison: *"Representative equity and procedural equity move
independently. Salinas Valley grants the strongest formal representation of the
three basins, then overrides it with a supermajority rule... Distributional
equity is the gap shared by all three basins. No basin reports which residents
bear dewatering risk."*

The synthesis adds that this is **not a data problem**: *"Existing data would
already support the analysis that the GSAs have yet to do,"* and §3.1.4 Gaps
confirms every basin holds the three required inputs (well-completion records,
published thresholds, community boundaries) and none runs the analysis.

**Required precision on Salinas Valley:** §3.1.3 states *"No document records a
failed vote, so this is architecture rather than cause."* The site must not
claim the supermajority rule has been used to block a decision.

---

### ✅ C-5 — "The literature–practice gap is itself a finding"

**Verdict: supported in specific, enumerable places — not as a blanket claim.**

Verified instances:

- §1.5 Gaps — per-well attribution tools exist in the literature; *"none of the
  three GSAs' documents shows evidence of applying, testing, or even citing
  this class of method."*
- §2.6 Gaps — the literature supplies methods for exactly the co-benefits the
  programs claim and cannot measure; one is in use in one basin, by a non-GSA
  partner, and is unadopted.
- §2.5 Comparison — *"None of the three documents a local validation of its ET
  product against field measurement."*
- §3.1.4 Gaps — every basin holds the inputs for a domestic-well protection
  analysis; none runs it.

**Counter-instances that must also be shown**, or the matrix becomes a
one-sided indictment:

- §2.9 Gaps — dust: *"the literature and the plans agree there is nothing"* —
  a method would have to be **built**, not adopted.
- §3.1.2 Gaps — community benefit units: *"This is a gap on both sides, not an
  unused method."*
- §3.2 Gaps — *"Every technique the literature recommends appears somewhere
  across the three basins."*
- §1.2 / §1.7 — the literature is largely silent on regulatory
  threshold-setting and on monitoring-network funding.

The Literature → Practice matrix must therefore carry a **"gap on both sides"**
state alongside "demonstrated in literature, not found in reviewed documents."
